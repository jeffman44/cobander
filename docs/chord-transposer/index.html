    How the transposer reads your chart

    A line counts as a chord line when most of what is on it are chord names (G, D/F#, Em7, Cadd9, Bbsus4, F#m7b5…). Those chords move; every other line — lyrics, section names, notes — is left exactly as you typed it. Bar lines like | G | D | are fine.

    Moving up 12 half steps lands back on the same key, so the transposer stops at 11 either way. Sharps or flats are picked from the new key (F, B♭, E♭, A♭ and D♭ get flats) unless you choose one.

    How many half steps?

    Two half steps is one whole step: G to A, or C to D. A singer who strains on the high notes usually wants the song one or two half steps lower; three or more changes the character of the song, so try it with the band before you commit.

    Questions

    Does it change the lyrics?

    No. Only lines made mostly of chord names are changed.

    Which chords does it understand?

    Any root A–G with sharps or flats, plus minor, 7, maj7, sus, add, dim, aug, power chords (5) and slash chords like D/F#.

    How does the capo calculator work?

    It lists the open-shape keys (C, G, D, A, E, or Am, Em, Dm) that reach the key you want with a capo at fret 7 or lower.
